A fitness gift works when it fits how a man actually trains. A heavy dumbbell set for someone who goes to the gym three times a week takes up space for nothing, and an expensive smartwatch for someone who doesn't track anything ends up in a drawer. Figure out where and how he trains first — then choose.

## Where Does He Train?

- **At the gym.** The equipment is already there, so focus on things that make each session more comfortable: a good bag, a bottle, lifting accessories, earbuds.
- **At home.** Compact equipment that replaces part of the gym: adjustable dumbbells, a kettlebell, a pull-up bar, resistance bands, a quality mat.
- **Outdoors.** Running, cycling, workouts in the park: a tracker or sports watch, a running belt, a reflective vest or light, bands that fit in a pocket.
- **He's thinking about starting.** Don't buy heavy equipment "for motivation" — it rarely works. A few sessions with a trainer, a gym pass or a set of bands and a mat is a gentler start.

## Gifts for Home Workouts

- **Adjustable dumbbells** replace a whole rack of weights. Check the maximum weight: for most men 20–25 kg per dumbbell is enough for a long time. Quick-change models are convenient but cost much more than classic plate-loaded ones.
- **A kettlebell** — one is enough for a full workout. For a beginner, 12–16 kg is a typical starting weight; for someone with training experience, 20–24 kg.
- **A pull-up bar** — a doorway model needs no drilling, but check the width of the door frame first. A wall-mounted bar is sturdier and allows more exercises.
- **Resistance bands** — cheap, light and good for warm-ups, assistance in pull-ups and travel. Buy a set of several strengths.
- **A mat** at least 6–8 mm thick for floor work; thicker and softer for stretching.

## Gifts for the Gym

- **Lifting straps, chalk, a lifting belt** — only for those who lift heavy and know what they're for. A belt must be chosen by waist size.
- **Training gloves** — a matter of taste: many experienced lifters don't use them, so ask first.
- **A sports bag** with a separate compartment for shoes and wet clothes.
- **A shaker bottle** or a quality water bottle that won't leak in the bag.
- **Sports earbuds** that stay put during movement and survive sweat (look for at least IPX4).

## Recovery

Recovery is often overlooked, and that's exactly why these gifts are appreciated:

- **A foam roller** — a simple, inexpensive tool for loosening tight muscles.
- **A massage gun** — popular after training; choose one with several attachments and a quiet motor. Not suitable for people with certain conditions, so if he has health problems, he should check with a doctor.
- **A massage or spa session** — a pleasant experience gift after a tough training block.

## Gadgets

A **fitness tracker or sports watch** is useful if he runs, cycles or likes numbers. Check compatibility with his phone and decide what matters: GPS for running, heart rate accuracy, battery life. For a man who just lifts weights, a tracker gives little — a training log app or a few sessions with a coach is better value.

## Bottom Line

Ask yourself one question: where and how does he train? For the gym — things that make training more comfortable. For home — compact equipment that replaces the gym. For a beginner — something easy to start with rather than heavy equipment "for the future". And when in doubt, a gift card to a sports store or a gym membership is a perfectly good choice.
