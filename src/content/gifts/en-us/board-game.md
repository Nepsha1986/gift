A good board game is one of the rare gifts that pulls a teenager away from the screen — and not because someone told them to. When a game clicks, friends come over "for half an hour" and leave at midnight. The trick is picking the right one: Monopoly gathering dust in the closet is not what we're after.

## First, Answer Three Questions

1. **Who will they play with?** With one friend, with a big group at parties, or with the family? The number of players matters more than anything else.
2. **How long do they have?** A 15-minute party game and a two-hour strategy game are completely different gifts.
3. **What do they already own?** Take a quick look at their shelf or ask a sibling — duplicates are the most common board game mistake.

## Choose by Type of Company

| Situation | Game type | Proven hits |
|---|---|---|
| Big group, parties | Party games | Codenames, Dixit, Exploding Kittens, UNO |
| 3–4 friends, an evening | Strategy "gateway" games | Catan, Carcassonne, Ticket to Ride, Splendor |
| Two players | Duel games | 7 Wonders Duel, Jaipur, Patchwork |
| A team that hates losing to each other | Co-op games | Pandemic, Forbidden Island |
| Fans of mysteries and escape rooms | Detective and puzzle games | Exit: The Game, Unlock!, Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective |

## A Bit More About Each

- **Party games** are the safest choice for someone who's new to board games. The rules take two minutes to explain, a round lasts 10–20 minutes, and they work for 4 to 8 people. Codenames is about word associations and clever hints; Dixit is about beautiful, strange pictures and imagination; Exploding Kittens is pure chaos and laughter.
- **Gateway strategy games** are the next step after Monopoly. In **Catan** you trade resources and build settlements, in **Carcassonne** you lay tiles and grow a medieval map, and in **Ticket to Ride** you build railway routes across a continent. Each takes 30–90 minutes and has enough depth to stay interesting for years.
- **Duel games** are perfect for a teen with a best friend, a girlfriend or boyfriend, or a sibling. 7 Wonders Duel is often called one of the best two-player games ever made.
- **Co-op games** have everyone playing together against the game itself. Great for families and for groups where competition tends to end in arguments.
- **Escape-room games** like Exit and Unlock! are one-time puzzles for an evening: you solve riddles, crack codes and race against the clock. Some Exit boxes even need to be cut and folded, so they can't be replayed — but that's part of the charm.

## Before You Buy

- **Check the age and player count on the box.** "10+" and "2–4 players" are not decoration.
- **Watch a 5-minute rules video** on YouTube — you'll immediately see whether the game fits their temperament.
- **Look at the BoardGameGeek rating.** It's the largest board game database in the world, and a score above 7 is a good sign.
- **Think about expansions.** If they already love a game, an expansion for it is a perfect "second step" gift.
- **For 16+**, a quality poker set or a classic chess set with a good board is a stylish alternative.

## Bottom Line

The best board game for a teenager is the one that fits their company: party games for big groups, gateway strategy games for evenings with friends, duels for two. Find out what's already on their shelf, watch a quick rules video — and you'll give not just a box, but dozens of evenings with friends.
