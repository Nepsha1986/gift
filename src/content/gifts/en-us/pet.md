A pet can be one of the most meaningful gifts in a teenager's life — and one of the most serious. Unlike any other gift, it can't be put on a shelf: a dog lives 10–15 years, a cat up to 20, and the teen will probably move out long before the pet grows old. So this is a decision for the whole family, not a surprise.

## Never a Surprise

- **Discuss it with the parents** if you're not one of them. A pet affects the whole household: who feeds it when the teen is at school, who pays the vet, who looks after it during holidays.
- **Discuss it with the teen.** Wanting a puppy and being ready to walk it twice a day in any weather are different things. Talk through a normal day with the pet.
- **Check for allergies** in everyone who lives in the home, and check the rules of the rental agreement or building.

## Which Pet Fits Which Teen

- **Dog** — the most demanding option: walks two or three times a day, training, someone at home or a plan for long absences. Best for an active teen and a family ready to share the care. Breed matters: energy level, size and health issues vary a lot.
- **Cat** — more independent, no walks, but needs a litter box, scratching post, attention and regular vet visits. Lives a long time.
- **Rodents (guinea pigs, rats, hamsters)** — less space and time, but they're not "toys": guinea pigs are social and should be kept in pairs, rats are clever and affectionate but live only 2–3 years, hamsters are nocturnal and often bite if woken.
- **Fish** — an aquarium is a hobby in itself: you need to understand water parameters, filtration and cycling the tank before adding fish. Good for a teen who likes systems and technology.
- **Reptiles** — a corn snake or a leopard gecko can be a great choice for a calm teen, but they need a terrarium with proper heating and lighting and a specific diet. Read up before buying.

## What It Really Costs

The pet itself is often the smallest expense. Think about:

- **The starting kit:** cage, aquarium or terrarium, bed, carrier, bowls, litter box, toys.
- **Regular costs:** food, litter, vaccinations, parasite treatment.
- **The vet:** sterilisation, unexpected illnesses and accidents. Exotic animals need a specialist vet, who isn't available everywhere.
- **Holidays:** a pet sitter, a boarding facility or relatives who agree to help.

## Where to Get a Pet

- **Shelters and rescue organisations** have animals of all ages; staff know their characters and will help you pick a suitable one. An adult animal with a known temperament is often easier for a first-time owner than a baby.
- **A responsible breeder** if you want a specific breed: they show the parents, provide documents and don't sell puppies or kittens younger than 8–12 weeks.
- **Avoid markets and random ads** — sick animals and fake pedigrees are common there.

## How to Give It

If the family has decided but you still want an element of surprise, give the "promise" rather than the animal: a leash, a bowl or a book about the breed with a card saying that you'll choose the pet together. This way the teen takes part in the choice, and the pet arrives in a prepared home rather than under the Christmas tree in the middle of a noisy party.

## Bottom Line

A pet is a good gift only when the whole family is ready for it and the teen has really thought about the daily care. Choose the animal by lifestyle and available time, not by how cute it looks, budget for the vet, and choose together. Then a pet becomes a friend for many years — and a real lesson in responsibility.
