A watch is one of the few gifts a man can wear every day for years — or never take out of the box. His phone already tells the time, so a watch today is about character: what he does, what he wears, whether he likes small mechanical things. A few observations before you buy make all the difference.

## First, Look at His Wrist

- **Does he already wear a watch?** Note its size, shape and strap — it tells you more than any guide. If he hasn't worn one for years, start with something simple and light.
- **Wrist size.** Most men are comfortable with a 38–42 mm case. On a slim wrist anything from 44 mm looks bulky. Thickness matters too: a thin watch slips under a shirt cuff, a chunky one doesn't.
- **Lifestyle.** Office and suits, workshop and sport, or jeans every day? A watch that's afraid of water and knocks won't survive a man who's always fixing something.

## Mechanism: Which One to Choose

- **Quartz** — accurate, inexpensive and practically maintenance-free; the battery is changed every 2–3 years. The best choice if he simply wants a watch that works.
- **Mechanical (automatic)** — winds itself from wrist movement, needs no battery, and often has a transparent caseback where you can see the movement. A great gift for someone who loves how things work. Keep in mind: it drifts a few seconds a day, stops if left unworn for a couple of days and needs servicing every 5–7 years.
- **Solar quartz** — charged by any light, so he'll never have to think about a battery.
- **Smartwatch** — a separate story. It must be compatible with his phone (an Apple Watch only works with an iPhone), needs charging every day or two and becomes outdated in a few years. Great if he tracks workouts and sleep; not great as a keepsake.

## Style

- **Dress watch** — thin case, simple dial, leather strap. For suits and special occasions.
- **Field or diver's watch** — legible, durable, water resistant. Goes with jeans, a jacket and even a suit, so for most men it's the safest bet.
- **Chronograph** — stopwatch sub-dials and a sporty look; busier and usually thicker.

A quick note on water resistance: **3 ATM (30 m)** means splashes only, **10 ATM** — safe for swimming, **20 ATM** — suitable for diving.

## Strap or Bracelet

- **Leather** — classic, but wears out quickly from water and sweat.
- **Steel bracelet** — durable and versatile, but needs to be sized. Most stores and watchmakers will remove links in a few minutes; keep the extra links.
- **Fabric (NATO) or rubber** — cheap, comfortable, easy to change.

A spare strap in a different style is a nice addition: one watch becomes two.

## Budget and Where to Buy

You don't need a luxury brand. Well-known manufacturers offer reliable quartz and automatic watches at modest prices; above that, you're mostly paying for the name and finishing. Avoid no-name fashion watches that imitate expensive ones — they look good in photos and fail within a year. Buy from an official retailer: you get a warranty and avoid fakes.

## Personal Touch

Engraving on the caseback — a date, initials or a short phrase — turns a watch into a family heirloom. Just remember that an engraved watch usually can't be returned, so check the size and style first.

## Bottom Line

Start from what he already wears: size, style, how active he is. If you're unsure, a simple 38–40 mm field watch with a steel case, a quartz or automatic movement and an easily replaceable strap suits almost anyone. Keep the receipt — and let him know he can exchange it.
