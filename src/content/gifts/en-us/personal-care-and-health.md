Many men take care of themselves with a minimum of tools: a razor, a bar of soap, toothpaste. That's exactly why a good care or health gift can be truly useful — it makes everyday routines a little more pleasant. The main thing is to replace something he already uses with a better version, not to give him a whole new routine he doesn't want.

## Start With His Habits

- **Does he shave or wear a beard?** This defines half of the gift options.
- **What's in his bathroom now?** A toothbrush with frayed bristles or a razor that pulls — upgrading these is a guaranteed win.
- **Are there problems he complains about?** Sore back after work, poor sleep, dry skin in winter — a gift that solves a real problem is valued more than any set.

## Grooming

- **Electric shaver** — foil models shave closer and suit those who shave daily; rotary models adapt better to the contours of the face and coarse stubble. Wet & dry models can be used with foam and in the shower.
- **Beard trimmer** — for a beard or stubble, look for a model with a precise length adjustment (in 0.5–1 mm steps) and a long battery life. A good trimmer with attachments also handles the neckline and body hair.
- **Beard care kit** — oil, balm and a comb make a long beard softer and neater. Choose a neutral or light scent.
- **Basic skin care** — a gentle face wash and a moisturiser with SPF. Most men don't buy these themselves but quickly get used to them.

## Health

- **Electric toothbrush** — oscillating-rotating and sonic models both clean better than a manual brush. Useful features: a two-minute timer and a pressure sensor; the rest is optional. Check the price of replacement heads — you'll be buying them for years.
- **Water flosser** — a good addition for those with braces, implants or gum problems.
- **Blood pressure monitor** — a practical gift for a man over 40 or anyone with pressure problems. Choose an upper arm model rather than a wrist one: it's more accurate.
- **Smart scales** — show not only weight but also an estimate of body composition; useful for those who are losing weight or training.

## Relaxation and Recovery

- **Shiatsu neck and back massager** — helps with tension after a day at a computer or behind the wheel.
- **Massage gun** — for those who train or have tight muscles.
- **Massage or spa certificate** — for those who never treat themselves.
- **Sleep accessories** — an orthopedic pillow, a sleep mask, a white noise machine. If he complains about poor sleep, this may be the most valuable gift.

## How Not to Get It Wrong

- **Fragrances and cosmetics are personal.** If you don't know his taste, choose unscented or neutral products.
- **Don't give "hints."** A weight loss device or anti-hair-loss shampoo as a gift can hurt, even if you meant well. Health gifts are better when he has mentioned the problem himself.
- **Medical devices aren't a replacement for a doctor.** A blood pressure monitor or scales are good for monitoring, but not for self-diagnosis.

## Bottom Line

The best care gift is a better version of what he already uses every day: a sharper trimmer, a toothbrush with a timer, a massager for a sore back. Start from his habits and complaints, avoid strong fragrances and hints, and the gift will be used every day rather than gathering dust on a shelf.
