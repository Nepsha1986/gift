Tickets are a gift of memories rather than things: nothing to store, nothing to dust. But with a concert or a play, more can go wrong than with a sweater — the date, the seats, the company. Here's how to make sure the evening turns out the way you imagined.

## Choose the Event Around Her

- **Listen for hints.** A favorite artist on tour, a play she mentioned, an exhibition she saved on social media — the best ideas come from her own words.
- **Her taste, not yours.** Unless it's a shared date, pick what she would choose, not what you'd like to see.
- **Format matters.** A standing fan zone at a rock concert and a seat in a concert hall are completely different experiences. The same goes for a four-hour opera versus a 90-minute stand-up show.

## The Date Problem

- **Check her schedule discreetly.** Ask her partner or a close friend, or simply say "keep Saturday the 14th free" without revealing why.
- **Look for flexibility.** Museums, exhibitions and many theatres sell open-date tickets or gift certificates — a good option if you can't guess the date.
- **A distant date is fine.** A ticket to a concert six months from now is a double gift: the event itself and months of anticipation.

## One Ticket or Two?

Almost always two. Going to a concert alone is rarely fun. Either you go together (say so when you give the gift), or the second ticket is for whoever she wants to bring.

## Seats and Logistics

- **Seats.** In a theatre, the middle of the stalls or the front of the balcony often has a better view than the side seats in the front rows. For concerts and stadiums, check the seating plan before buying.
- **Getting there and back.** If the event is in another city, add train tickets and a hotel — and it becomes a mini-trip. If it ends late, think about a taxi home.
- **Buy only from official sellers.** The venue's box office or its official ticket operator. Resale through marketplaces and messengers carries a real risk of fake or duplicate tickets. Some events issue personalized tickets — check whether her name is needed.

## Make It Feel Like a Gift

An e-ticket is just a QR code on a screen. Make it special: print it and put it in an envelope with a card, or pair it with something related — a vinyl record or a T-shirt of the band, opera glasses for the theatre, a team scarf for the match, an exhibition catalog.

## Beyond a Single Evening

- An annual museum membership.
- A season subscription to the philharmonic or theatre.
- A festival pass.
- A guided tour or backstage tour.

## Bottom Line

The perfect ticket is to an event she has hinted at, on a date she's free, with good seats and someone she wants to share it with. Buy from official sellers, take care of the logistics — and present it nicely, so the gift feels special long before the lights go down.
