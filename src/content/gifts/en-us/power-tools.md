A power tool is a gift that gets used for years — if it's the right one. Someone who already has a well-equipped workshop doesn't need a second drill, and someone who hangs a shelf once a year doesn't need a professional rotary hammer. A few things to find out before you buy.

## First, Check His Battery Platform

This is the most important tip. Most modern cordless tools are sold within a "platform": one brand's batteries fit all tools of the same voltage line (usually 18 V or 20 V max — essentially the same thing). If he already has a drill of a certain brand, buy a tool from the same brand and line — he'll be able to use his existing batteries and charger.

That also means you can buy the **"bare tool"** version (without battery and charger) — it's significantly cheaper. If he has no tools yet, buy a kit with a battery and charger and choose a brand with a wide range of tools in its line: this will be the foundation for future gifts.

## What to Give Depending on What He Does

- **Basic home repairs** (shelves, furniture assembly, curtain rails): a **cordless drill/driver** is the first tool to have. If he already has one, an **impact driver** is a great addition for long screws and assembling furniture without effort.
- **Drilling concrete and brick:** an ordinary drill struggles here. You need a **hammer drill** for occasional jobs or an **SDS rotary hammer** if he's renovating.
- **Woodwork:** a **jigsaw** for curved cuts, a **circular saw** for straight cuts in boards and sheets, a **random orbital sander** for finishing. For someone who builds furniture, a **router** opens new possibilities.
- **Metal, tiles, cutting rebar:** an **angle grinder**. A powerful and dangerous tool: give it only to someone with experience, ideally with a set of discs, gloves and protective glasses.
- **Car and garage:** a cordless **impact wrench** for wheel nuts, an **inflator** for tyres.
- **Small jobs and electronics:** a **cordless screwdriver** with a set of bits or a **rotary tool** with attachments for engraving, grinding and cutting.

## What to Pay Attention To

- **Brushless motor** — more efficient, lasts longer and gives more running time per charge. Worth paying extra for.
- **Battery capacity:** 2 Ah is lighter and enough for a drill; 4–5 Ah for saws, grinders and long jobs.
- **Corded or cordless:** cordless is more convenient; corded is cheaper and makes sense for tools that run for a long time in one place (sanders, miter saws).
- **Warranty and service:** buy from an official retailer. Cheap no-name tools often have weak batteries and no spare parts.

## Useful Additions

Sometimes the best gift isn't a new tool but what makes the old ones better: a set of quality drill bits for wood, metal and concrete, a set of bits for the driver, an extra battery, a tool bag or organizer case, safety glasses and hearing protection, a good spirit or laser level.

## Bottom Line

Find out which brand and battery platform he already uses and what kind of work he does most often. A tool from the same line or a quality accessory set is almost always a hit. If you're unsure, a gift card to a hardware store lets him pick exactly what's missing in his workshop.
