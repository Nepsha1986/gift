Choosing clothes for a man is like playing the lottery: you need to guess the size, the fit, the color and his ideas about style. Many men are conservative and will keep wearing their favorite old sweater even when you give them a new, "better" one. But it's possible to win — **if you know which things are risky and which are almost a guaranteed hit.**

## The Golden Rule: Accessories Are Safer Than Clothes

Here's a simple risk map:

| Risk level | What to give | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Wallet, belt, scarf, gloves, socks, tie, cufflinks | Size doesn't matter or is easy to guess |
| Medium | Sweater, hoodie, T-shirt, pajamas, robe | Size is needed, but a loose fit forgives mistakes |
| High | Jeans, suit, jacket, shoes | Exact fit is needed — better to go shopping together |

## Low-Risk Gifts That Always Work

- **Leather wallet.** Take a look at his current one: does he carry a lot of cards or a few? Cash? A slim **cardholder** suits those who pay by phone, while a classic **bifold** suits those who carry cash and receipts.
- **Belt.** Measure his current favorite belt from the buckle to the hole he uses — that number is his size. Choose the buckle to match his usual style: a simple classic for the office, a massive one for jeans.
- **Warm scarf and gloves.** Merino wool or cashmere are soft, warm and don't itch. Gray, navy, camel — colors that go with any coat.
- **Quality socks.** It's no joke: a set of good merino or bamboo socks, or a bold pattern, is something that men genuinely appreciate but rarely buy for themselves.
- **Sunglasses.** Look for **UV400** protection — it blocks harmful ultraviolet. Polarized lenses remove glare from water and the road, which drivers and fishermen value. The shape is personal, so a gift certificate from an optician is a good alternative.
- **Watch.** A classic that never goes out of style. Pay attention to the size of the case — 38–42 mm suits most wrists.

## Clothes: How Not to Miss the Size

- **Check his wardrobe.** Look at the labels on the things he wears most often — not the newest ones. Take a photo of the label: it shows the size and often the brand he likes.
- **Ask an accomplice.** His mother, sister or a close friend often knows his size better than he does.
- **Choose a loose fit.** Hoodies, sweaters, pajamas and robes forgive mistakes; fitted shirts and jeans don't.
- **Size up if in doubt.** Something a little large can be worn; something too small never will be.
- **Keep the receipt** and agree on an exchange in advance. Put a note in the box: "If the size is wrong, we'll exchange it — no offense taken."

## Style: Respect His Taste

- **Look at what he actually wears**, not what you'd like him to wear. If he's into jeans and hoodies, he won't suddenly start wearing a tweed jacket.
- **Upgrade, don't revolutionize.** The best idea is a better version of something he already loves: the same hoodie, but from good cotton; the same sneakers, but a new model.
- **Neutral colors** — navy, gray, black, olive, beige — are easier to combine and hit the mark more often than bold experiments.

## When It's Better to Shop Together

Jeans, suits, jackets, shoes and boots require trying on. Instead of guessing, give a **"day of shopping"**: a certificate from his favorite store + you as a style consultant + lunch together. It's a gift and a date at the same time.

## Bottom Line

If you're not sure about the size, choose an **accessory** — a wallet, belt, scarf or good socks. If you want clothing, find out the size from his wardrobe, choose a loose fit and neutral colors, and keep the receipt. And for jeans, suits and shoes, the best gift is shopping together.
